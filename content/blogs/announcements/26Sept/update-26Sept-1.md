Thank you for your continued use and support of Open Endfield Map.

At 9:00 a.m. on September 2 (UTC+8), we completed the update for the new map version. After the game servers opened at around 10:30 a.m., OEM's most active Pioneers uploaded more than 450 screenshots within 3 hours, providing full screenshot coverage for the new version's markers. Thank you to everyone who contributed screenshots and shared collection tips with the community!

In addition, for Intel that cannot be collected directly through the map, we launched the OEM Intel Hub. The page first went live early last month and works with Open Endfield Assistant (OEA) to help you quickly organize your Intel collection progress. Visit [**OEM Intel Hub**](https://oem.re/intel) to view acquisition methods and related tips for each Intel entry and keep track of what you have yet to collect.

Below are the latest improvements to the map.

## Marker Paths and Related Hints

![Figure 1: Marker Paths and Related Hints](/blogs/announcements/26Sept/fig1.gif)

For collectible objectives that cannot be fully represented by a static marker, OEM now displays animated paths or related-location hints when you select the relevant marker:

* Selecting an **Aurylene** marker draws its complete movement path on the map, with animated direction cues to help you identify its range and the locations it passes through.
* Selecting **Guiding Butterflies** or their related crate displays the complete guiding path, the locations of each stage along the way, and the final target crate. The map also identifies the link between the butterflies and their crate.
* Selecting a **Locked Crate** displays location hints for all three fragments required to unlock it, making them easier to find nearby.
  
## Intel Archive Collection Page

![Figure 2: Intel Archive Collection Page](/blogs/announcements/26Sept/fig2.webp)

The new [**OEM Intel Hub**](https://oem.re/intel) is now officially available. It brings together the acquisition methods and collection status of all six in-game Intel categories. You can search by Intel name, filter by its in-game category, and expand each entry to view specific requirements such as missions, shops, or map interactions.

For Intel obtainable on the map, you can also open its corresponding OEM marker directly from the collection page. When signed in to your OEM account, your Intel progress is synchronized through OEM Cloud so you can continue tracking it across devices.

## Open Endfield Assistant

[**Open Endfield Assistant (OEA)**](https://github.com/Logical-Byte/open-endfield-assistant) is an open-source Intel scanning tool for Windows. OEA automatically navigates the in-game Intel Archive, recognizes collected Intel titles with OCR, and corrects recognition results against game data.

After the scan is complete, select **Export to OEM** in OEA to open the OEM Intel Archive collection page in your browser and import both collected and uncollected states, without checking every entry manually. OEA currently supports Windows 10 and 11 on x86_64 systems. You can [download the latest release](https://oea.oem.re/latest) and follow the project instructions to get started.
